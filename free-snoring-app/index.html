Free Snoring App for iPhone: What Free Really Gets You

Every popular snoring app is free to download, and most are not free without limits. The usual pattern: recording and a score are free, listening to your own snoring clips or keeping more than a few nights is paid. Snorly breaks that pattern on purpose: recording, playback of every clip, the score, the timeline and your whole history are free every night, with no account and no night limit. Premium only adds extras.

Key facts
  • "Free, ad-free, good: pick any two" is the most-upvoted answer on the r/snoring thread about free snore apps (Reddit, 2024).
  • Apple's Voice Memos is the only truly free option, at the cost of an 8-hour file of roughly 200–250 MB at the default compressed setting that you must scrub through yourself (user measurements; lossless runs to about 2 GB).
  • Snorly's free plan has no night limit and no ads; Premium (subscription or one-time lifetime) adds full-night audio, remedy comparisons, the PDF report and Apple Health import.
  • SnoreLab's free version plays a limited number of recordings per night and, after the first 5 sessions, cannot run on 2 consecutive days (SnoreLab FAQ, 3 September 2026).
  • Prices below are as shown in the US App Store on 2 September 2026 and change by country.

What does "free" mean in a snoring app?

Free means the App Store price is $0 and the app works on the first night. It does not mean every feature is included. The features most often held back are playback of the recorded clips, history beyond a few nights, trend charts and export. That is not a scam, it is how a one-person or small-team app pays for itself without ads. The honest question is not "is it free" but "what will I be able to do tomorrow morning without paying".

Is Snorly free?

Yes, and not as a trial. Recording, playback of every clip, the 0–100 score, the night timeline, breathing flags and your full history are free every night, with no night limit and no ads. Premium is only for the extras: full-night audio, factor and remedy comparisons, the PDF report for a doctor and Apple Health import. When Voice Memos is the right free tool

If you want a single yes-or-no answer and do not mind the work, Voice Memos does it. Start a recording, put the phone on the nightstand, plug it in, and in the morning drag through the waveform looking for the tall spikes. Budget a few hundred megabytes of storage and 15 minutes of scrubbing per night. A snore app does the scrubbing for you and keeps only the clips with a time stamp, which is the part you will want on night two. What to check before you trust any free app

  1. Where the audio goes. Snorly processes and stores audio on the phone only; read the privacy label of any app that mentions the cloud.
  2. Whether playback is free. If you cannot listen to your own clips, you have a score, not proof.
  3. Whether it records only snoring or the whole night. Whole-night files are heavy and hard to review.
  4. Battery and Do Not Disturb. Any app that listens all night should be plugged in.

Related questions

Is SnoreLab free?

SnoreLab is free to download and use, with limits. Its FAQ (checked 3 September 2026) says the free version records the night, shows the morning charts and stats, plays a limited number of recordings per night and lets you tag remedies and factors. After your first 5 sessions the free version cannot be run on 2 consecutive days. Premium removes the limits and adds the BreathFlow breathing index, access to all past sessions and export.

What is the best free snoring app?

For automatic clips, a score and free playback with no night limit, Snorly. For a fully free app from a remedy brand, SoundSleep. For a one-off yes-or-no with no app at all, Voice Memos. "Best" depends on whether you want to scrub an 8-hour file yourself.

Do free snoring apps have ads?

Some do. Snorly has no ads in any tier. Reddit threads on free snore apps repeatedly complain about ads and permission requests, so read the privacy label before installing.

Can a free app detect sleep apnea?

No app, free or paid, can diagnose sleep apnea. Some, including Snorly, flag breathing patterns worth showing a doctor. See snoring vs sleep apnea.

Snorly is a snore recorder and tracker, not a medical device. It does not diagnose sleep apnea or any other condition. If you notice pauses in breathing, gasping or daytime sleepiness, talk to a doctor.
