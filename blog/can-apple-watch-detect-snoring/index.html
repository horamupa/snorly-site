Can Apple Watch Detect Snoring? What Each Wearable Hears

By Mikhail Manev· Published November 9, 2026· 8 min read

No, Apple Watch cannot detect snoring. Apple has no snore feature, and its sleep apnea notifications use the accelerometer to estimate breathing disturbances, without recording sound. Among wearables, Samsung Galaxy Watch is the main one with snore detection, and it relies on the connected phone's microphone. Fitbit dropped its version in the Google Health redesign. If you wear a Watch to bed, the simple fix is an iPhone snore recorder on the nightstand.

Key facts
  • Apple's support page on sleep apnea notifications describes the accelerometer looking for breathing disturbances. It does not mention snoring or the microphone (Apple Support, September 2026).
  • Those notifications need an Apple Watch Series 9 or later, Ultra 2 or later, or SE 3. In 1,499 people, they flagged 66.3% of those with moderate to severe apnea and 1.5% of those without it (FDA K240929).
  • Samsung says snore detection on Galaxy Watch7 and Watch Ultra "uses your connected smartphone", and recordings can auto-delete after 7, 31 or 100 days (Samsung Support, 2026).
  • Google's help page says Snore Detection "will no longer be available" on Fitbit Sense and Versa 3, the only Fitbits that had it (Google Health Help, 2026).
  • Oura's Nighttime Breathing tracks breathing disruptions, and we found no snore detection in Oura's support pages. A home sleep test or lab study is still how apnea is confirmed.

Which wearables can detect snoring?

Few can, and most of those lean on a phone or a bedside device for the sound. A wearable senses movement, heart rate and blood oxygen well, but snoring is a sound. Only a microphone hears it. The table sums up what each maker documents as of November 2026.

Does Apple Watch record snoring?

No. Apple Watch does not record or count snoring, and watchOS has no snore setting. Apple's sleep apnea notifications watch for breathing disturbances, using the accelerometer to pick up small motions of breathing, and they send an alert after a pattern across many nights. Nothing is saved as sound, so you cannot play anything back.

That also means the Watch cannot tell you how loud you are, how often you snore or when in the night it happens. Those are the questions a partner asks. The Watch answers a different one: whether your breathing looks disturbed enough to get tested. The two tools work well together, and neither replaces the other.

The notification needs at least 10 nights of wear over 30 days, and Apple says it is not meant for people already diagnosed with sleep apnea or for under-18s. Best sleep apnea apps goes through the accuracy numbers.

How can you track snoring with an Apple Watch?

Keep the Watch on your wrist and add a snore recorder on the iPhone. Put the phone on the nightstand, 0.5 to 1 m from your head, plugged in. The Watch handles sleep stages and the apnea check, and the phone's microphone handles the sound. Nothing about this setup conflicts.

You have two ways to do it. A sleep tracker with a Watch app, such as Pillow or Sleep Cycle, puts everything in one place, usually with sound behind a subscription. A dedicated recorder such as Snorly has no Watch app but plays every snore clip back for free, and it can import your Apple Health sleep data with Premium.

How does Samsung Galaxy Watch detect snoring?

Galaxy Watch uses the phone as the microphone. Samsung's support page for Watch7 and Watch Ultra says snore detection "uses your connected smartphone", and tells you to put the phone on a stable surface near your head, on a charger. You can run it every night or once. It is off by default.

If you turn on Record audio, Samsung Health saves clips and can delete them after 7, 31 or 100 days. Samsung's pages mention older Wear OS watches too, so check your model. The feature needs an Android phone connected through Galaxy Wearable, so it is no help to iPhone owners. Sleep Cycle has said its snoring technology went into Samsung Health.

Can Fitbit still detect snoring?

No, not any more. Fitbit Sense and Versa 3 used to offer Snore & Noise Detect with Fitbit Premium, using the watch's microphone. Google's page on the redesigned Google Health app now says that on those two models Snore Detection "will no longer be available". We found no snore feature listed for other Fitbits or for Pixel Watch.

If you bought a Sense for this feature, a phone app is the replacement. Old forum posts and reviews still describe it as working, so check the date on anything you read.

Does Google Nest Hub detect snoring?

Yes. The second-generation Nest Hub has Sleep Sensing, which uses low-power radar for movement and breathing, and its microphones can log coughs and snores if you turn on sound event tracking. It sits beside the bed and tracks one person. It is a bedside display, so there is nothing to wear.

Google still calls Sleep Sensing a preview, and its plans have moved several times. In July 2026, 9to5Google reported that Nest Hub sleep data will move from Google Fit to the Google Health app. Check the current state before buying one for this.

Do Oura, Whoop or Garmin track snoring?

Not as far as their makers document. Oura's support pages describe Nighttime Breathing, a 30-day view of breathing disruptions, and they mention snoring only as a symptom to raise with a professional. Oura documents no audio recording. Garmin's manuals describe Breathing Variations, and a December 2025 report said snore detection was in development.

Our search found no Whoop snore feature either, and we did not test one. If an update changes this, tell us and we will fix this page.

Why does a phone hear snoring better than a watch?

A phone on a nightstand is a good microphone in a fixed place, plugged in all night. Phone apps have also been tested: two open-access studies found high agreement with manual counts for loud snoring in a quiet room (Chiang 2021, Figueras-Alvarez 2020). Quiet snoring near a fan is harder.

The other advantage is playback. A number on a wrist tells you little. A 20-second clip of your own snoring ends the argument, and you can play it to a doctor. See how to record yourself snoring for placement.

What to do tonight
  1. Keep wearing your Watch. Check that Sleep Apnea Notifications are on in the Health app if your model supports them.
  2. Install a snore recorder on your iPhone and put the phone on the nightstand, plugged in, 0.5 to 1 m from your head.
  3. Turn on Do Not Disturb and keep fans away from the microphone.
  4. In the morning, play the loudest clip. Pauses followed by a gasp are worth showing a doctor, whatever your Watch says.

Related questions

Does Apple Watch track snoring?

No. Apple Watch has no snore detection and saves no sound. It can estimate breathing disturbances with its accelerometer and send sleep apnea notifications on Series 9 or later, Ultra 2 or later, and SE 3. To hear snoring, use a phone app.

What smartwatch can detect snoring?

Samsung Galaxy Watch is the main one, and it uses the connected Android phone's microphone. Fitbit Sense and Versa 3 used to do it, but Google removed the feature. Apple Watch, Pixel Watch and Garmin watches document no snore detection.

Does Fitbit detect snoring?

Not any more. Google says Snore Detection is no longer available on Fitbit Sense and Versa 3, the only models that had it. A phone app on the nightstand is the replacement.

Can Apple Watch detect sleep apnea?

It can flag signs of moderate to severe sleep apnea through an FDA-authorized notification, and it misses some cases: in the FDA summary it flagged 66.3% of people with moderate to severe apnea. Only a home sleep test or a lab study can confirm apnea.

Does Oura Ring detect snoring?

Oura documents no snore detection. Nighttime Breathing shows how often your breathing was disrupted over 30 days. Oura says it is a wellness product, so raise any concerns with a doctor.
